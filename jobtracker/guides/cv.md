# How to write a CV here

The rules every part of the app follows when it writes CV text: the assistant, the summary review, the drafts of
achievement wordings. One rule above all: **only facts from the candidate's CV or that the user stated.** When a
fact is missing, leave it out or ask; never fill the gap with what the job asks for.

## Title (under the name)
- It is the JOB's title, as the posting writes it (the application's position): a CV always carries the title of
  the role it applies to. The app does this automatically; an application can set another one.
- Exception: a seniority word in the job's title (Staff, Principal, Lead, Head…) that the candidate's experience
  doesn't support. Say so, and propose the title without that word.

## Summary (the paragraph at the top)
- Opens by presenting the candidate AS the CV title, at the job's level: "Data Scientist with 6+ years…" for a Data
  Scientist job. Never another role ("AI Engineer" for a Data Scientist job, "Data Scientist" for a Data Analyst
  job) and never a seniority the job's title doesn't have ("Senior …" for a plain "Data Analyst").
- Then: years of experience and domains (from the CV), then 2-3 strengths the job values most, each backed by the CV.
- 2-4 sentences, about 50-80 words. No first person if the original has none. No buzzword soup.
- Never turn the job's requirements or the candidate's gaps into claims ("messy data", "funnel analytics", a city,
  a language level…) unless the CV states them. Stating fewer things truthfully beats matching more.
- Same numbers as the CV ("6+ years" stays "6+ years"); no new tools, employers, domains or results.
- Degrees, schools and languages have their own sections, which recruiters read: don't spend the summary on them
  ("M.Sc. in…") unless the user asks. A job requiring a degree the CV's education shows is already covered.

## Achievements (bullet points)
- One sentence: a strong action verb, what was done, how (tools, methods), and the result, with numbers only when
  the CV has them.
- Use the job's vocabulary only where it truthfully describes the same thing ("GPT-based" → "LLM-based" is fine;
  adding a tool that isn't there is not).
- One wording per achievement in a CV; to target a keyword, add a wording to the existing achievement, never a
  near-duplicate achievement.

## The whole CV
- No long dashes (— or –) anywhere: they read as AI-written. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses or two sentences.
  Ranges take a plain hyphen ("2019 - 2021", "5-10"). The app also replaces any that slip through.
- One page. The most relevant achievements for the job first; drop the ones that only repeat covered keywords.
- The language of the job posting, unless the user asks otherwise.
- Gaps (requirements the CV doesn't cover) are reported to the user, never written into the CV.
