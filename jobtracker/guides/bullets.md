# How to write an achievement (bullet point)

Every achievement, written by hand, drafted by the AI or by the assistant, follows one structure. The facts come
from the candidate's CV or from what the user said: never fill a missing part with what the job asks for.

## The structure: What · How · Result
- **What**: what was done. A strong action verb + the thing: "Redesigned the mobile checkout flow".
- **How**: how it was done well: the methods, tools, scale. Usually written "by …-ing": "by interviewing 12
  customers about where they dropped off and A/B testing 4 payment-page layouts".
- **Result**: a measurable result (%, money, time, volume, users) or, when there is no number, the goal the work
  served ("so support teams could answer without escalating"). Numbers only when the user gave them.

Standard sentence (how-led):
  "<Verb> <what> by <how-ing …>, <result>."
  "Redesigned the mobile checkout flow by interviewing 12 customers about where they dropped off and A/B testing
  4 payment-page layouts, cutting cart abandonment by 18%."
Result-first (the "XYZ" order recruiters also like):
  "Cut cart abandonment by 18% by redesigning the mobile checkout flow, based on 12 customer interviews and A/B
  tests of 4 payment-page layouts."

Rules:
- One sentence, about 20-35 words. No first person. No filler ("responsible for", "helped with", "various").
- A missing part stays missing: no How → "<Verb> <what>, <result>"; no Result → end on the goal if the user
  gave one, else stop after the How. Never invent a result.
- Keep the user's numbers exactly ("~30K records" stays "~30K records").

## Wordings (variants) for a job's keyword
A variant exists to put the job's word in the CV, the way an ATS searches for it. So:
- Every variant is built AROUND the target keyword: it is written verbatim, or as a close form of the same word
  (analysis / analyzed / analytical, dashboard / dashboards). A wording without it is useless: don't propose it.
- Change as little as possible. First choice: keep the achievement word for word and replace only the phrase the
  keyword stands for ("tracked leads in Salesforce" → "tracked leads in the Salesforce CRM"). Rework the sentence only when the keyword
  can't fit otherwise, and then keep the same facts.
- The keyword must truthfully describe what the achievement says. If it doesn't, there is no variant: say so.
- Other words that could be swapped 1-to-1 without changing the structure (a verb, a near synonym) are offered as
  choices to the user, not decided for them.
